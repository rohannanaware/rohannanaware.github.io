> You need to accept a person for who they are and not who you want them to be

I try my best to be a good listener and in that process I’ve been all ears to people confessing about their relationships, how they are handling it and where they think it’s headed, or how a proposal went and why it went the way it did. Rarely do I give any advice on what should be done because I’m not the best person to help there. But I do give them examples from others on anonymity and allow them to draw parallels.

Few elements repeatedly occur in relationships striving to stay strong or proposals that get rejected. And this article’s intention is to capture a few of them. Again, on complete anonymity as always :)

## There’s nothing called love at first sight

It’s a term made famous by romantic books and movies. But I beg to differ here. You can’t know enough about a person in a few minutes, days, weeks or even months to understand their true liking, inhibitions, insecurities, interests. There will always be common interests among two people, but that’s not enough to work as a foundation for relationship.   
You need to give more time to understand each other before committing. Be friends, do things you like together, bounce off ideas, have debates even fights, anything that’ll help you get to know each other better.  
Over time you’ll have your answer on whether or not you want to be more than friends.

## Respect

Never take a person in your life for granted. Everyone’s time runs per the same clock. Respect it. Everyone’s feelings are equally important and fragile as yours, appreciate them and try not to hurt. Everyone has a unique way of living, a unique outlook towards life, unique interests, unique imperfections and that’s what makes them beautiful. It’s not in our right to dictate any change in it. Live and let live. Always suggest, never command, and command if and only if it’ll benefit the person to grow as an individual in a positive way. If the person is not harming himself or the society then it’s not in your right to intervene in his/her way of life.

## Space and freedom

Let me put this straight, you don’t own the person you’re in a relationship with. Hell, you don’t own anyone except yourself. Everyone deserves and desires to live their life the way they find fulfilling and unless it’s causing harm to that person or society it’s not in your right to intervene. I wrote an [article](https://link.medium.com/dMug4M56V3) on this earlier.   
Allow the other party to continue living the way they used to before committing to you. That’s part of what makes them who they are.

Treat people the way you would want to be treated.

## Everyone has better things to do, always

If you stumble across a person who’s not in a relationship then it’s not because they can’t find someone, rather they may have chosen to be so. Maybe they don’t have bandwidth or are not ready or simply not interested. They can always be cool to hang out and chat but that doesn’t command an approach from your end. And even if you do approach, they have all rights to speak out the truth and put your proposal down.

## No one’s into giving hints

Most times people are preoccupied with their own set of problems, challenges, life decisions, work or even mental debates. What I’m trying to say is most people don’t have time to give you hints that they like you. Nothing like that exists. If a guy likes a girl, he’ll eventually say that to her face and vice versa. Until that happens it’s wrong of you to make any assumptions.   
Expectations on an imaginary footing can cause severe damage.

### Jotting down few scenarios -

When people say something, they mean exactly that, there’s no hidden meaning in casual chatter.  
If she likes your shirt, she just likes your shirt, if she likes your hairstyle, she just likes your hairstyle. Same applies for her liking your trekking stories, your casual attitude, your bad jokes, your discipline, your diet regime, your figure, your writing, your drawing, your coding skills, your gym routine, your song playlist.   
If she asks for help during workout, then she just needs help during workout. If she offers to cook for you because your cooks on leave, then she just wants to cook for you. If she travels an hour just to give you the home made sweets she got from her hometown, that just means she wants to give the home-made sweets to you. If she asks you out for lunch every day, that just means she likes your company over lunch, same applies for trek or weekend outings, if she consoles you in your depression then she just cares about your mental health. There’s nothing more and nothing less to it. That’s how simple things are.  
If she likes you, she’ll say that to your face. Don’t assume! Same applies for women too.

## Looks don’t matter in long run

You see a hot girl and you assume she’s either looking for relationship or is taken. What if she has taken an oath of celibacy? What if she’s a monk? What if she just likes to dress up well for herself and not others. Please don’t judge people by the way they look. Applies for both genders.

Relationships thrive on mutual understanding — knowing each other’s good and bad side and accepting it as part of that person. Knowing that no one is perfect and being at peace with that fact.

I’m not saying that good looking people don’t have strong relationships. But what I’m trying to say is universally the reason relationships remain strong is more to do on how good you are intrinsically than extrinsically.

## It’s not always about sex

A friend of mine shared a great [article](https://medium.com/@GoodMenProject/the-one-thing-men-want-more-than-sex-98a801833e39) visiting this topic. Men aren’t wolves and women aren’t needy. There’s more to life than love and sex.  
What makes relationships last is the emotional support you give and receive. If being with your partner gives you a sense of calmness and protection, then that’s the person you want to be with. And this applies to both genders.

## Bring logic not ego to your fights

Arguments are always welcome in a relationship. They are like tests. And the way you solve these tests can have major effect on the strength of your relationship. More often than I like, I’ve seen good people ruining their relationship over a small argument by bringing their ego in it.   
Look at an argument as a common problem to solve. Add supporting points on either side, avoid being judgmental, weigh the pros and cons and solve it together, like the way you would solve a cross word puzzle. The argument would soon turn from a headache to a problem both of you are enthusiastic to resolve.

## Running away is never the right choice

Whatever may be the state of your relationship be bold enough to critically assess it together. I have seen toxic relationships ruin one or both of the parties. It’s not the right choice to stay in such relationship but it’s also not right to run away from it either. Face your fears, put forth the argument and close it logically. Both parties should get equal support and protection to voice their thoughts. Bring in your friends and family if you need support, there’s always someone who cares enough to help you through any situation.

Similarly, it's not right to run away from a one-sided relationship either. It'll devastate the other party. Put forth the arguments, have a logical discussion, be clear on why it's not working out. Make sure you empathize and close on a logical note. More often than not you'll end up staying best friends for life.

## How to know she's the right person?

Simple answer, she motivates you to be a better person than what you are currently.

Relationships are thought to be fragile, but I personally feel if executed well they are one of the strongest emotions human brain can produce. It’s upto us on how things turn out in the end. Always.
